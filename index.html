Why another training app

Most programs are written for somebody else.

A plan from a magazine, a PDF from a friend, a list generated by a chatbot — they all assume a gym you may not have, a body that never gets sore, and a week that never changes. Three things go wrong, every time.

🏚️

It assumes equipment you don't have

Half the plan needs a cable machine. Your gym has two. You improvise, the stimulus changes, and nobody tells you whether the swap still works.

📈

"Just add weight" ignores what happened

Adding load after a session where you missed reps on three sets isn't progression. It's debt — and it shows up two weeks later as a stall.

🎲

You never find out why

A plan that can't explain itself can't be corrected. Why squats, when you said your knee hurts? Silence isn't an answer.

How it works

Answer, train, progress.

Tell it about you

Goal, experience, which gym you're training at, muscles you want to bring up, anything that hurts, how many days a week, how long a session can run, and which split you like. Under two minutes.

Get a program with reasons

Exercises, sets, reps and rest for every training day — plus a plain-language note explaining why each choice was made and what was left out.

Train with the app open

Warm-up sets computed from your working weight, a rest timer between sets, last session's numbers right under the exercise, and a load suggestion at the end — only when you earned it.

Inside the generator

No black box. No dice.

Kaha Trainer doesn't ask a language model what you should train. It runs a set of rules over a catalog where every exercise carries a grade — per goal, per role, per prerequisite. Same answers, same program, every time.

That matters for one practical reason: a program that is reproducible is a program that can be argued with. If something looks wrong, there is an actual reason to point at.

  • Graded catalog. Every exercise is scored for each goal and tagged by role — primary compound, isolation, core — plus the overlap group it belongs to.
  • Volume from the literature. Weekly sets per muscle start in the 10–20 range, lower for beginners, who grow on less and recover worse from a lot.
  • Pain is a filter, not a preference. Flag a joint and the loaded patterns come out of the pool entirely.
  • Prerequisites respected. Advanced lifts don't show up in a beginner's first program just because they score well.
  • Rest days are part of the plan. Fixed weekdays, or a cycle — four on, one off. The calendar reflects what you actually committed to.
  • Warm-ups are computed. Percentages of your working load, descending reps, rounded to plates that exist on a real bar.

Progression

It only tells you to add weight when you actually earned it.

At the end of a session, Kaha Trainer looks at what happened. Every working set completed, every rep target met, and reps still in reserve on the sets before the last one — then it suggests a heavier load, in a jump that fits the plates you have.

One set short of target and it says nothing. Progressing on an unmet goal is how a stall gets built.

Milestones that accumulate

Total volume lifted, workouts completed, calories burned — six tiers each, across your whole history.

A weekly goal and a streak

Set how many sessions a week counts as a good week. The current week only breaks the streak once it's over, not while you're still in it.

Apple Health, both ways

Finished workouts go to Health with duration and energy. Height, age and body data come back to sharpen the calorie estimate — and when a number isn't known, the app shows a dash instead of guessing.

Privacy

Free, ad-supported, and straight about what that means.

Kaha Trainer is free and carries ads. That's the deal, and it's worth stating plainly rather than burying in a policy nobody opens.

Usage stats are anonymous

Product analytics are tied to a random identifier generated on your device — not to your account, your email, or a single measurement you've logged.

Ads never interrupt a set

No full-screen ad during an active workout, none over the rest timer, and none between finishing and seeing your summary. That's enforced in code, not by policy.

Health data stays put

What the app reads from Apple Health is used on the device for calorie and body calculations. Your account stores what you choose to save in the app.

FAQ

Questions worth asking first

Is it really free?

Yes. No subscription, no paid tier, no locked features. The app is supported by ads.

Do I need an account?

You can explore the app — the catalog, the screens, how it all works — without one. Creating a program, logging a workout or saving a measurement needs an account, because that data lives in the cloud so it survives a new phone. Sign up with an email address or with Sign in with Apple.

Is there an Android version?

Not today. Kaha Trainer is an iPhone app, built with native iOS frameworks including Apple Health.

Does it use AI to write my program?

No — and that's a design decision, not a limitation. The program comes from explicit rules over a graded exercise catalog, which means the same answers always produce the same program and every choice can be traced to a reason.

What if my gym is missing a machine?

Register the gym and check off what it has. The generator only prescribes exercises you can perform there, and you can keep more than one gym — home and commercial, for instance — and switch between them.

Can I build a program myself instead?

Yes. The assistant is one of two routes; the other is building from scratch, picking every exercise, set and rep yourself. You can also save your own training splits and reuse them.

Which languages does it speak?

English, Portuguese (Brazil) and Spanish — the whole interface, not just the menus. It follows your iPhone's language by default, and you can override it in the app.
