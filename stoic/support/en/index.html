Support

We answer every email. Usually within two business days.

Write to info@kahatrainer.com
To get a useful answer faster, include your iPhone model, your iOS version, the app version (at the bottom of the Profile tab), and what you expected to happen.

Common questions

Do I need an account?

No. You can use the quotes, journal, paths, challenges and library as a guest, and everything stays on your iPhone. Create an account — with Apple, Google or your email — to back up your journal, use it on another device and talk with the AI.

I want to delete my account

In the app, go to Profile → Delete account. The account and all its synced data are erased immediately, and the data on the phone is cleared.

My journal isn't on my new phone

Sign in with the same method you used before (Apple, Google or the same email). Entries written in guest mode were never uploaded and stay only on the old phone.

The AI chat isn't working

The chat needs an account and your agreement to the notice shown before the first conversation. Free accounts get a few messages to try it; the No ads + AI plan includes a fair daily and monthly limit. If you reached a limit, the chat tells you when it resets.

Am I really talking with Marcus Aurelius?

No. Replies come from an AI (Claude, by Anthropic) that speaks in the style of each Stoic, based on their writings. It can make mistakes, and it is not therapy. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (US) or your local emergency number.

How do I cancel or restore a subscription?

Subscriptions are managed by Apple: iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions → Eudia. Cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date. On a new phone, open Profile → Plans and tap Restore purchases.

I'm not getting reminders

Check the times under Profile → Reminders and that notifications are allowed in iPhone Settings → Notifications → Eudia. Focus modes can also hold them back.

Where do the texts come from?

The classic texts and quotes come from public-domain translations — George Long for the Meditations, the Enchiridion and the Discourses, and Richard Gummere for Seneca's letters. The full list is under Profile → Credits.

I found a bug

Please tell us. Steps to reproduce it are worth more than anything else you can send — what you tapped, what you saw, and what you expected instead.
